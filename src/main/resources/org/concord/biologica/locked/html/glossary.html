BioLogica Genetic Glossary


These terms will be referred to throughout the BioLogica Curriculum and in genetics.  Each term is explained in general and in terms of BioLogica dragon genetics.  

Allele: A particular version of a gene at a location in a chromosome.   In dragons, the Horns gene is located in Chromosome 1.  There are two alleles for Horns, H and h.  Mutations can create new alleles.

Autosome:  A chromosome other than a sex chromosome. Dragons have two different autosomes, Chr1 and Chr2.

Autosomal Characteristic: Characteristics that are determined by genes in any chromosome except the sex chromosomes.

Characteristic: A feature that can be inherited.  Examples of dragon characteristics are Horns, Wings, and Color.

Chromosome: A coiled rod-shaped structure made of DNA and protein. The DNA contains a linear arrangement of genes. The chromosome is visible with a microscope when the nucleus/cell divides.  A single set of dragon chromosomes consists of two autosomes (Chr1 and Chr2) and one sex chromosome (X or Y). Dragon body cells contain a pair of these sets.

Co-dominance: The phenotypic expression of both of the two alleles in a heterozygote. There are no dragon examples of  co-dominance. But in humans, the blood type AB results from co-dominance between two alleles called IA and IB.

Dihybrid Cross: A cross in involving two pairs of contrasting traits.  A dihybrid cross in dragons might involve a 2-legged, fire-breathing dragon and a 4-legged, non-fire-breathing dragon.

Diploid: Having two sets of chromosomes.  In dragons, body cells are diploid.

DNA: Deoxyribonucleic acid. The genetic material of most organisms.

Dominance: The phenotypic expression of only one of the two alleles in a heterozygote.  The correct term is complete dominance. The allele that is expressed is called dominant.  The allele that is not expressed is called recessive. In dragons, horns, wings, and fire display complete dominance.

Dominant: When one allele of a heterozygous pair of alleles is expressed in the organism, that allele is dominant.  The other allele is the recessive allele.

Eukaryotic Cells: Cells with nuclei.

Fertilization: When a sperm (or a pollen in plants) unites with an egg.  Since sperm and eggs each contain one set of chromosomes, fertilization produces a cell containing two sets of chromosomes.  In dragons, fertilization produces  a cell containing two sets of three chromosomes.

F1 Generation: The offspring of a particular parent pair. F2 generation is the offspring of matings of the F1 generation.

Gametes: Male (sperm or pollen) and female (eggs) sex cells.  They are usually the products of meiosis. In BioLogica, they are the things that you drag into the fertilization window to create a baby.

Gene: A hereditary unit.  A segment of the DNA that makes up a chromosome.  Examples of dragon genes are Horns and Wings.  Color1 and Color 2 are each genes.

Genotype: The total set of genes located in the nucleated cells of an organism.

Haploid: Having one set of chromosomes.  In dragons, gametes are haploid.

Heredity: The transmission of traits from parents to their offspring.

Heterozygous: When there are two different alleles for a gene in a diploid. For example, Hh.

Homozygous: When there are two of the same alleles for a gene in a diploid. For example, HH or hh.

Incomplete dominance: When the heterozygous phenotype is in between (intermediate to) the homozygous phenotypes.  In dragons, legs display incomplete dominance.  Homozygous dragons have either four or zero legs.  Heterozygous dragons have two legs. There are three traits, but only two alleles.

Meiosis: The process of nuclear division that reduces the number of chromosomes, and so the amount of genetic material, by half. One nucleus starts with two sets of chromosomes.  It produces four nuclei, each with one set of chromosomes.  These products are usually gametes. In BioLogica, this is what happens in the Meiosis Window.

Mitosis: The process of nuclear division that maintains the same number of chromosomes, and so the same amount of genetic material.  The growth of multicellular organisms like dragons is the result of mitosis, starting from the fertilized egg.

Monohybrid Cross: A cross involving a single pair of contrasting traits.  Monohybrid crosses in dragons might involve horned and hornless dragons or plain or fancy tail dragons.

Mutation: A change in the DNA that can be inherited.

Pedigree: Chart showing how traits are passed from generation to generation in a family.

Phenotype: The external or detectable appearance of an organism. In humans, hair color is external and blood type is detectable. All of the phenotypic traits in dragons are external.  

Polygenic inheritance: When more than one gene contributes to a particular characteristic.  In dragons, Color is polygenic since two genes, Color1 and Color2, contribute to it.

Punnett Square: A method used to establish the probabilities of the results of a genetic cross.

Recessive: When one allele of a heterozygous pair of alleles is not expressed in the organism, that allele is recessive.  The other allele is dominant.

Sex Chromosomes: The chromosomes that determine the sex of an individual; not autosomes. Dragons with two X chromosomes (XX) are female.  Dragons with an X and a Y chromosome (XY) are male.  Birds and most reptiles are the opposite, with XY for females and XX for males.

Sex-Linked Characteristics: Characteristics that result from genes in sex chromosomes. In dragons, there are three X-linked genes -- Fire, Color1, and Color2, so specific Fire and Color characteristics are X-linked traits.

Single-gene Cross: A genetic cross that involves a single gene. For example, crossing a horned dragon and a hornless dragon.

Trait: Specific versions of a characteristic.  Dragons can either have the horns or the no-horns trait for the Horns characteristic.

Two-gene Cross: A genetic cross that involves two genes.  For example, crossing a 2-legged, fire-breathing dragon and a 4-legged, non-fire-breathing dragon.  Also known as a Dihybrid Cross.
